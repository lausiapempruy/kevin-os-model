# Before/after examples

These examples demonstrate editorial choices, not a universal style template. The "better" version is only appropriate when its factual content fits the situation.

## Generic school-topic opening

**Before**
> In today's modern era, education plays a very important role in shaping the future of every individual. It is not just about gaining knowledge, but also about building character and preparing for success.

**After**
> School teaches subject knowledge, but it also gives students practice working with others, managing deadlines, and solving problems.

**Why**
The revision names specific functions instead of repeating broad claims. Use only the examples that fit the assignment.

## Promotional product copy

**Before**
> Experience a seamless and revolutionary platform that takes your productivity to the next level.

**After**
> Keep project notes, tasks, and deadlines in one place.

**Why**
The revision describes what the product does. Add a benefit claim only if the product actually provides it.

## Overlong explanation

**Before**
> It is important to note that, due to the fact that the system is designed in such a way that it has the ability to automatically save the user's work, users do not need to worry about manually saving their progress.

**After**
> The system saves work automatically, so users do not need to save it manually.

**Why**
The edit removes redundant phrasing without changing the meaning.

## Fake confidence

**Before**
> This method guarantees perfect results every single time.

**After**
> This method can make the process more consistent, but results still depend on the input and setup.

**Why**
The revision removes an absolute promise that would require unusually strong evidence.

## An important exception

A technical manual, legal document, scientific paper, or school assignment may need formal wording and explicit signposting. Do not make it casual just to avoid AI-sounding patterns. Precision matters more than stylistic novelty.

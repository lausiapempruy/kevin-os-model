# Anti-slop text review checklist

Use this when the user asks for a detailed audit, an editorial review, or a final quality pass.

## Meaning and evidence
- [ ] The central claim is clear.
- [ ] Facts, figures, names, dates, and quotes are preserved accurately.
- [ ] No unsupported claim, citation, or personal detail was added.
- [ ] Uncertainty and qualifications remain intact.
- [ ] Examples are relevant and grounded.

## Structure
- [ ] The opening reaches the actual subject quickly.
- [ ] Each paragraph has a distinct job.
- [ ] Claims are supported rather than repeated.
- [ ] Headings, bullets, and tables improve navigation.
- [ ] The ending adds a conclusion or practical implication; otherwise it stops naturally.

## Style
- [ ] Generic filler and empty importance claims are removed.
- [ ] Transitions express real relationships between ideas.
- [ ] Inflated adjectives and unsupported superlatives are removed.
- [ ] Sentence rhythm is natural for the genre and writer.
- [ ] Punctuation and formatting are not distracting.
- [ ] Tone matches the intended audience and the user's voice.
- [ ] Slang, jokes, typos, or first-person details have not been forced in.

## Task fit
- [ ] Requested language, length, format, and level of editing are followed.
- [ ] Technical terms and required wording are retained.
- [ ] The rewrite is no more invasive than needed.
- [ ] The result is readable aloud and easy to understand.
- [ ] No claim is made that the text is guaranteed to pass an AI detector.

## Reporting findings
For audits, prioritize findings by impact:
1. Meaning or factual problems.
2. Structure and repetition.
3. Specificity and tone.
4. Sentence-level polish.

Use examples from the actual draft. Avoid vague feedback such as "make it more human" without identifying what should change.
